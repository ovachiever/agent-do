## Evidence lanes

**Lane 1 — literature.** Each entry cites its study. Key sources, all checked at
source 2026-09-08:

- Juzek & Ward 2024, "Why Does ChatGPT 'Delve' So Much?", arXiv 2412.11385 — 21
  focal words overrepresented in LLM output; "delves" rose 0.21 → 14.38 per
  million in scientific abstracts 2020–2024 (+6,697%), "intricate" 6.22 → 44.22
  (+611%). Cause analysis points at RLHF, not training data or architecture.
- Kobak et al. 2024/25, "Delving into LLM-assisted writing in biomedical
  publications through excess vocabulary", arXiv 2406.07016 / Sci. Adv. — 379
  excess style words in 15M PubMed abstracts; excess frequency ratios: delves
  r=28.0, underscores r=13.8, showcasing r=10.7; excess gap words: potential
  (δ=0.052), findings (δ=0.041), crucial (δ=0.037).
- Liang et al. 2024, "Monitoring AI-Modified Content at Scale", arXiv 2403.07183 —
  in ICLR 2024 peer reviews: meticulous 34.7-fold, intricate 11.2-fold,
  commendable 9.8-fold increases in sentence probability.
- Reinhart et al. 2025, "Do LLMs write like humans?", PNAS / arXiv 2410.16107 —
  LLM-likely words: delve, foster, crucial, underscore, meticulous; plus the
  grammatical layer (atlas).
- Pangram, "9 Signs of AI Writing, Backed by Data" (pangram.com/signs-of-ai-writing)
  and "Introducing Pangram's AI Phrases" (blog/pangram-ai-phrases) — per-10k-word
  multipliers: markdown 12x (bold alone 43x), AI phrases 12x, em-dashes 10x
  (OpenAI models 9x human baseline), bullet lists 9x, triads 4x, "not just X but
  Y" 3x, "Certainly! Here's"-style headers 70x, checkmark emoji 167x,
  box-drawing characters 940x; "complex tapestry" 82x.
- Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing — the community catalogue: era-tagged AI
  vocabulary, puffery formulas, negative parallelism, rule-of-three,
  copula-avoidance, vague attribution.

**Lane 2 — contrastive (computed here).** Smoothed log-odds and per-10k rates

## What the lexicon cannot catch

Per the Pangram dossier, the detector's signal is learned, not enumerable: a
draft can pass every table above and still scan as AI. [MEASURED] "Humanized AI
text still exhibits the same repetitive phrases as non-humanized AI text"
(DAMAGE, arXiv 2501.03437). The layers beyond lexicon — syntactic uniformity,
burstiness, discourse templates, idiolect absence, per-family signatures — are
the atlas's territory. This file is the floor, not the ceiling.

## Appendix A — linter run on our own drafts (2026-09-08)

`python3 scripts/lint-ai-speak.py drafts/*.md`: all nine files **clean** — zero
Tier 1 hits, zero Tier 2/3 over-rates. Sanity check: the same linter raises 28
flags on the 1,052-word generic-AI sample (10 Tier 1, 16 Tier 2, 2 Tier 3). The
2026-08-31 voice rulings already stripped the lexical layer from our drafts.

What the contrastive study found in the drafts instead, i.e. where the residual
AI signal must live:

- [COMPUTED] **Refrain templating across essays.** The strongest AI-weighted
  trigrams in drafts-vs-human are our own repeated closing-ask formula: "reply
  and tell me", "that's what we do here", "cause that's what" each appear 6x
  across the draft set. Within one essay a refrain is craft; the same refrain
  shipping in every essay is a per-author signature a detector can learn. A
  single-file linter cannot see this; flagged for the adversarial loop.
- [COMPUTED] **Human-weighted markers we underuse.** The strongest
  human-weighted n-grams are involvement features: contractions ("I don't",
  "we're"), "a lot (of)", "really", "need to", first-person planning ("so I
  can", "I need to"). Matches Reinhart: LLMs run low on hedges, discourse
  markers, personal asides. Drafts sit closer to human here than the generic
  sample, but Erik's session rate is the target.
- [COMPUTED] **Em-dash irony.** Erik's certified corpus uses em-dashes at
  8.5/10k — above Paul Graham (3.5) and quadruple Pangram's human baseline (2).
  The house ban makes drafts scan less like Erik on this one axis while dodging
  the 17/10k AI tell. Cheap disambiguation surrendered; noted, not contested.
- [COMPUTED] **Questions are not a tell for us.** Erik 49/10k, PG 40/10k,
  drafts 62/10k, generic 47/10k. Rates overlap; only the self-answered kind
  discriminates (Tier 3).

## Vocabulary v2: measured register evidence

The deep word hunt (`mn-dc5728`) supplies a full ranked inventory of observed
words, recurrent two-to-four-word phrases, and the declared construction
counters. See `voice/word_hunt/README.md` for the fixed protocol, source
provenance, data limitations, and reproduction commands. Each entry carries
counts, word denominators, document support, model-family contrasts, and its
writing register. The complete observed inventory also preserves singleton
phrases; those sparse phrases are excluded from the ranked review list.

The generated `lexicon-v2-candidates.json` is a hashed advisory feed. Use it
through the existing linter with `--word-hunt <artifact> --register essay`
(or `forum` / `agent_chat`). It reports repeated terms above the matching
source rate separately from house-rule violations. These descriptive ranks
create no new automatic bans and make no authorship verdict. Public essays
and forum posts are attributed to named accounts, not certified as unaided
human writing. The certified Erik register retains its own denominator.
