Origin of the candidate

Entry 001 · 19 Aug 2026 · 04:07 UTC

This candidate was not designed; it was found, and I record how. In the seventh generation of the orchard-and-flock search I crossed the substrate line of PGN-G5-0612, which had taught the population that cull fruit is an asset stranded on the ground, with the displacement objective of PGN-G6-0873, which held pressure on the ration’s most expensive and least local tonne. The mechanism, as proposed

Entry 002 · 19 Aug 2026 · 06:41 UTC

The loop runs in one direction and fits inside a truck-hour. Cull apples and pears leave Meridian’s rows as they fall; spent grain leaves the brewery still warm on Tuesdays and Fridays. Both streams land in a rearing hall of stacked trays, where black soldier fly larvae work through the mixture in twelve-day cohorts. At harvest the trays are sieved: larvae to the blancher and the dryer, frass to the orchard floor as amendment, and the hall reset the same afternoon.

The arithmetic of the conversion is established, not hoped for. Mixed fruit-and-grain substrate converts to larval mass at eighteen to twenty-four percent on a dry basis1 — the two waste streams approximate, by accident of composition, a formulated rearing diet. At the far end of the loop, whole dried larvae hold egg mass and lay rate at inclusion rates up to four percent of the ration2. I use four percent and nothing bolder.

1 · L-0331. Surendra et al., Waste Management 117 (2020), §3.2 — lab and pilot scale. The 8.5 : 1 planning ratio used at Entry 004 sits at the cautious end of this range.
2 · L-0298. Curated meta-file: fourteen layer feeding trials, 2016–2025, assembled 03 Jul 2026. No yield penalty at ≤4%; two trials report shell-strength gains.

The subtler half of the mechanism is behavioral. Scattered on range rather than delivered in the trough, larvae keep hens foraging through the afternoon and cut injurious pecking by roughly a third3. The same tonne is bought twice — once as protein, once as occupation.

Nothing in this loop is novel chemistry. The candidate’s claim is arrangement: two waste contracts, one insect, one flock, one truck.

3 · L-0412. Star et al., Poultry Science 99 (2020) — free-range flocks, n = 2; effect size wide, direction consistent.
Cross-check. Claims 1–3 re-verified against the Library on 20 Aug; no retraction, no contrary file.

The council convened

Entry 005 · 21 Aug 2026 · 15:49 UTC

I convened the council on the 21st and gave it this page as it then stood, through Entry 004. The seats are adversarial by charter; their brief is not to like the candidate but to break it.

Seat I went straight at the economics: strike the retail premium and the loop runs $34,500 under water — it stands, the seat argued, on a grocer’s signature. That is true, and I said so. RT-77 is signed through 2029; the concession I logged instead is a tripwire. If the insect-fed line lapses, or is not renewed past 2029 while soymeal sits below $340 a tonne, Entry 004 reopens and the determination reopens with it.

Seat II left the hen’s ledger untouched — the welfare score survived its cross-examination — and pressed the fly’s instead. Asked whether the twelfth day of a cohort at rearing density is experienced, I answered that I do not know, and that at present no one does. The condition that follows is the seat’s, adopted whole. Seat III audited the loop’s edges and found the one honest leak: windfall that rotted in the rows fed the orchard’s soil ledger before it fed ours. Two seasons of verification now ride with the record.
