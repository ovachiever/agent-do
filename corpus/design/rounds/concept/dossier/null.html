The Mechanism

Commercial rations treat a laying flock as one average bird, and the average is set at peak lay. Past seventy-two weeks the bird has changed — egg mass has plateaued, absorption has slowed — but the specification follows her unrevised, and between 18 and 24 percent of the crude protein she ingests goes unused4: deaminated in the liver, excreted as nitrogen, and paid for three times over, in feed, in house ammonia, in manure hauled to fields that did not ask for it.

PLG-1847 replaces volume with timing. Albumen formation concentrates the demand for methionine into the eight hours after ovulation3, while overnight shell formation draws on almost none of it. The candidate lowers the base ration to 14.1 percent crude protein and returns the difference as free lysine, methionine, and threonine, micro-dosed through the waterline and phase-locked to each house’s recorded oviposition curve. The old hen’s slower absorption stops being a defect: the dose arrives in the hour her gut can use it2.

The idea is a recombination, not an invention. Parent PLG-0791 proved circadian split feeding but kept protein flat; parent PLG-0562 mapped late-cycle amino acid retention but never touched delivery. Generation 12 crossed them and proposed moving the amino acids out of the ration entirely. Across 4,200 simulated flock-years in eleven climates, hens on the pulse regime held lay persistency 1.1 points longer than controls while eating two and a half points less protein.

Superscripts count independent primary sources per claim. Nine stand under this section — strongest: Poultry Science 104(6), 2025; INRAE pulse-feeding trials, 2024–26. Thinnest: absorption kinetics past 90 weeks rests on two pre-2023 studies, flagged in the Library.
