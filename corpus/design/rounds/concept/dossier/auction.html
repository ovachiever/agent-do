Mechanism

Underlined claims carry their sources in the margin.

A laying hen descending from the top tier of a modern aviary drops as much as 170 centimetres in a single flight, and she absorbs the landing through a keel that evolution shaped for perching, not for impact. The Descent Lattice interposes staggered rails between tiers — spaced so that no transition in the house exceeds a 45-centimetre hop, angled along the 40-degree descent lines inherited from PG-0893, and sleeved in the micro-grip texture PG-1466 carried through pilot. Given the option, the birds take the stairs: offered landings within 45 centimetres, hens elect stepped descent over flight in 91 percent of transitions1.

1Stratmann et al., Appl. Anim. Behav. Sci. 205 (2018) 34–42; replicated in-house, behaviour probe B-334, n = 1,880 transitions.

The fracture arithmetic is an energy budget. Force-plate work places 78 percent of fresh keel fractures above the 25-joule impact band2 — energies a hen reaches only in free flight between tiers. Capping descent height caps kinetic energy at landing to roughly a third of the unmitigated case; biomechanics model v14 carries that cap through a full 80-week cycle and returns fracture prevalence of 29 percent against the audited 47 percent baseline (S-9917, CI 24–33). Nothing in the lattice asks the hen to change. It re-prices the choices she already makes.

2Toscano lab force-plate series (2020), impact energetics of keel contact; internal fit in biomech model v14 §3.

The welfare case and the economic case are the same case. A hen carrying a keel fracture lays about 14 fewer saleable eggs across a cycle3 and sheds part of her lay into the checked grade; at 100,000 hens housed, an 18-point drop in prevalence returns the annual lay of roughly eight hundred uninjured birds without adding a bird to the house. The estimate below prices only that recovery — lay, grade, and survival — and deliberately prices the welfare premium at zero.

3Rufener et al., Poult. Sci. 98 (2019); corroborated in partner grading logs F-102.
